### Provenance of the organic claim (why it is hedged)

The organic statement rests on a **single source** — the site-visit record, and within it, the farmer's own narration:

> `20260903_CEPOTX_site_visit_to_raimundo_geniza_farm.pdf` (visit to U-06-07, 3 Sep 2026):
> "Farm managed organically and **certified IBD NOP since 2006** (Raimundo's narration, IMG_8210)."
> "The sítio is **organically certified (IBD NOP) since 2006**; no pesticide or artificial chemical use observed on the premises (IMG_8210 narration, on-site walk)."

**No IBD certificate document is held in this repo.** The only certification artifacts on file are the FITO/GAP written-assurance letter (2024) and the DUNS/CNPJ records. The RFQ therefore states the claim as *reported* and requests the certificate itself before any organic claim is relied upon on import. Correspondingly, `entity.json` lists "IBD NOP organic" among certifications — that entry is likewise narration-sourced and should be treated as unverified until the certificate is received.

## 7. Market-access caveat (buyer-side, not stated in the RFQ)

Buyer-side research (Sep 2026) indicates **Brazil raw cocoa beans may not currently be cleared for import into mainland China** — Brazil appears on GACC's *green coffee* origin list but not the *unroasted cocoa beans* list. Hong Kong operates a separate regime. This is why the RFQ defers the destination/loading airport and why country-level market access should be confirmed with the China-side party (Ling) before freight is committed. Processed cocoa products (roasted / cocoa mass) are not subject to the same origin-list restriction.
